## What changed

- `lib/quota.ts` — Adds a quota decision function that chooses whether to continue, pause until a reset, or hold for a person.
- `extensions/quota-gate.ts` — Applies those decisions at agent boundaries and after usage-limit errors, including timed pauses and resuming work.

## Unfamiliar surface

- `setTimeout` — Node’s timer API; used to wait until the quota reset time, in chunks no longer than the maximum supported timer delay.
- `Promise<void>` and `await` — Model the pause as asynchronous work so the extension can wait for the reset without blocking Node’s event loop.
- `Promise` constructor — Creates a promise resolved by the timer; used to make `setTimeout` awaitable.
- `Date.now()` and `toLocaleString()` — Get the current time and format the pause-until time for a user-facing notification.
- `pi.appendEntry` — Adds a custom entry to the session; used in print mode to record that a continuation is needed when sending a user message is not appropriate.
- Discriminated unions and string-literal types — Represent the three quota outcomes and let callers branch on `action` while retaining outcome-specific fields.
- Default parameters — Supply empty overrides and the current time when callers omit them.
- `Array.some` — Checks whether an active override suspends a quota window’s threshold.
- `Math.min` and `Math.ceil` — Cap timer delays and round pause deadlines up to a whole second.
- `setTimeout` polling loop — Rechecks the clock after each timer, so long waits remain safe even when a timer’s maximum delay is shorter than the pause.
- `async` functions and `try/finally` — Ensure the shared pause is awaited and its `pendingPause` marker is cleared even if polling fails.
- `pi.sendUserMessage` — Enqueues a continuation in interactive mode after a usage-limit pause.
- `process.exit` — Ends headless operation when a quota hold cannot be resolved.
- `toISOString()` — Writes a machine-readable timestamp into a session entry.

## The alternative

The previous approach treated threshold exhaustion as a soft hold requiring confirmation. The new approach pauses automatically for a five-hour quota window and resumes after the reset plus a safety margin, while weekly exhaustion still requires a person. This keeps short-lived rate limits from unnecessarily blocking unattended work without making longer-term weekly limits self-clearing.

## Verify by hand

- Confirm a five-hour window with a future reset pauses until the reset plus five minutes, then polls again.
- Confirm weekly exhaustion creates a hold, while an active override suppresses the weekly threshold but not 100% exhaustion.
- Confirm a usage-limit error resumes interactively by sending `continue`, and in print mode records `pi-build-continue-needed` instead.

## To modify this yourself

Understand the quota window labels and thresholds in `lib/quota.ts`, then trace how `atBoundary` and the `agent_end` handler in `extensions/quota-gate.ts` apply decisions. Pay particular attention to the distinction between a timed five-hour pause and a weekly hold that requires user resolution.
